## Chassis
### The chassis serves as the outer shell of the platform, housing most of the electrical components while protecting them from dust, loose dirt, and mud. It acts as the primary load-bearing structure transferring pressure from the platform to the ground, which requires it to negate torsional forces on the body and prevent the frame from sinking into the soil. It consists of three distinct parts: the Body, the Cylindrical Exoskeleton, and the Foundation.

## 1. The Body
The body consists of two layers: the top layer protects the arms, while the bottom layer houses the majority of the electronics. Most of the IMU components will be contained within these compartments. The main hardware and power supply will be located specifically on the bottom layer; with approximately 8 liters of volume, it provides sufficient storage for all mission-critical equipment.
## 2. Cyllindrical Exoskeleton
The exoskeleton is implemented around the chassis to minimize material weight, thereby reducing manufacturing and transportation costs. This structure features a two-layer design utilizing cylindrical joints and trusses to link the components together. It alleviates the majority of the downward pressure exerted by the cargo and counteracts torsional forces to prevent the body from twisting.
## 3. Foundation
The foundation is an engineered interpretation of the Miura-ori origami pattern. It utilizes large, thin plating to distribute cargo pressure across multiple surface contact points on the ground, reducing manufacturing complexity and simplifying the engineering requirements of the exoskeleton. The foundation will be tested at sizes ranging from 0.75x to 1.5x the total width and length of the body.
